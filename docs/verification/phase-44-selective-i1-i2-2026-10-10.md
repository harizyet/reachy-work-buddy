## 1. I-1: what was built (source only; `knowledge/answerability_b1`, still imported by nothing in production)

| Change | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `ScopedFinding` (types) and a scoped-search reader in admission | A sentence of the form "Searched/Checked/Looked in <scope> only [for X] and found none there" is a **bounded** search; the scope is quoted from the record, and a following "<Y> was not checked/searched" sentence in the same paragraph supplies the unsearched part. Read **before** the relation's negation pattern, so a loose "no X" pattern can never turn a search result into an absence. No "only" (an unbounded "searched everywhere"), a hedge, or a competing subject means no scoped finding. |
| State function (existence asks) | authoritative (owner/system) negation → NEGATIVE_SUPPORTED, and a scoped note adds nothing to it; presence record → SUPPORTED (a search of one place that found nothing is consistent with it existing elsewhere); presence plus negation → CONFLICTED; a negation from a **non-authoritative** author (third party, attendee) → NEGATIVE_UNSUPPORTED, "attributed, not established"; scoped note only → NEGATIVE_UNSUPPORTED (scoped form); nothing → NEGATIVE_UNSUPPORTED (unknown form). Previously any author's negation gave NEGATIVE_SUPPORTED; the rubric v5 class 2 needs an authoritative record. |
| Renderer | scoped: "A search of the Osprey wiki found no runbook for Osprey [E1]; not searched: the shared drive. That does not show there is none." (or "the record does not say where else was checked"); several scoped notes are listed separately and never combined; attributed: "A record by a third party says there is no runbook for Osprey [E1], but that is not an authoritative source, so the records do not establish it." A scoped record with no evidence id fails closed to the unknown wording. |
| Readability (needed for I-2) | `compose(plan)`: answered parts first, then findings (conflicts, orderings, scoped searches), then one merged "the records do not say the A or the B" line; "a/an" by label; singular/plural for many-valued relations ("The attendees … are P, Q and R [E7]"), values with one shared source cited once; display forms Friday, August, GPU host; optional `unit` ("40 minutes") and `prefix` ("the edge host") on `RelationSpec`, display only; conflict text "one record says A [E1]; another says B [E2]". One existing assertion in `test_answerability_b1.py` was updated for that wording. |

**Tests** (`tests/test_answerability_scoped.py`, 27 new; B-1's 128 still pass): scope (quoted, unsearched part, no "only", hedged, competing subject, no composition, same scope in two records); the loose-negation trap; the three classes; **source authorization** (unauthorised, stale, wrong policy version, changed ACL revision and absent decision all give the empty-search wording **byte for byte**; an unauthorised authoritative absence does not establish absence; instruction-bearing notes excluded); **citation provenance** (exact record cited, missing evidence id fails closed, malformed provenance excluded, creation/retrieval time cannot change the wording); **conflicting evidence** (presence beats a scoped search, authoritative absence against presence is CONFLICTED with the scoped note out of it, another subject's or relation's scoped note ignored, attributed negation plus scoped); composition; articles; plural and shared citation; units and prefixes. A helper asserting "no world-level wording" is itself tested as non-vacuous. ### 2.2 Why 40 sub-claims are withheld (diagnosed, not tuned)

By relation: `default_model` 17, `default_model_history` 13, `decision` 8, `support_hours` 2. The dominant mechanism is **model names being in the known-subject list**: a model is a subject for "where does Swift-6B run" and a value for "default model" and "decision". A sentence that names it is then read as naming a competing subject (13), or its title/context binding is refused or the value skipped. This is also why the earlier extension record's 282/307 was higher: its harness kept backslashes in the known list (an escaped hyphen), so model names never matched. Replaying that harness reproduces **282**; a faithful list gives **267**. A caller-side fix (known subjects without model names for every relation except `runs_on`, and `co_subjects_ok` on the two model-valued relations that lacked it) recovers 15 of the 40 and gives **282 / 307 with 25 false abstentions and still 0 unsupported claims and 0 wrong values** (diagnostic, source unchanged, `DIAGNOSTIC_…` in the report). The remaining 25 are the earlier record's recall limits (cross-sentence references, free-text kinds, historical sentences with two values). Fixing this properly is a design decision (typed entity lists per relation), not done here.

### 2.3 Decomposition from question text (floor, not tuned)

Using only the record-side cues, `decompose` falls back on **124 of 173** questions and decomposes exactly **49**; 147 of 307 sub-claims are recovered. The deterministic path therefore cannot yet start from a free-text question; the gold-component results above assume the question was already typed. This is the largest gap to an end-to-end release.

## 3. What a mechanical pass does not establish (needs a human reader)

A closed-vocabulary or template pass shows that every typed value in a reply is an admitted fact with a cited, authorised record and that all code-written sentences are fixed templates. It does **not** show: that the sentence an answer was read from actually answers the question rather than agreeing with the gold by coincidence of wording; that a first-person statement ("I will fix the Cedar rollback test") is an assignment (speaker-map values are checked against the segment and speaker, not for that meaning); that a quoted scope is faithful beyond string containment; that the uncertainty wording ("That does not show there is none", "unclear, so I will not pick an answer") is the right amount for a lay owner; that a multi-part reply is understandable; or anything about relations without specs.

Single-reader pass (author; NO INDEPENDENT HUMAN REVIEW): about 55 composed replies across all eight question families, read before and after the readability changes. Defects found and fixed: "The attendees … is", "is edge host", "order of the … records" repeated, "a escalation", merged-line missing "the", conflict wording "one record says … one record says". Remaining observations, not fixed: text values lose their article ("Hopper settings"); "The records on the default model of Marlin are unclear, so I will not pick an answer" for history questions the ambiguity policy withholds says what happened but not why; multi-part replies put the answered parts first, which reorders against the question; no check of voice rendering. These need a decision on tone and are the natural content for the manual-review step in the plan.

## 5. Remaining failures and recommendation

Remaining failures: 40 false abstentions (diagnosed above; 25 after a caller-side fix), 124 of 173 questions that the decomposer cannot type, specs that are derived from the gold, 11 of 24 historical sub-claims answered, and the unfixed presentation notes in section 3.

**Recommendation: the deterministic path is not ready for formal acceptance, but its safety properties are strong enough to keep it as the primary release candidate.** On dev15 it makes no unsupported claim, no wrong-value claim, no citation or authorisation error, and every withheld, conflict, negative and ordering sentence is a fixed template; the scoped-search negative behaves as the contract requires on 64 fresh existence cases. What it lacks is coverage and a front end: a question decomposer that does not fall back on most free-text questions, a relation-spec source independent of the gold, a typed entity list so model names are not competing subjects, and cross-sentence/free-text recall. Suggested next steps, each needing approval: (1) the typed-entity fix and a spec registry; (2) a labelled decomposition set and question-side cues, measured before any acceptance run; (3) re-run I-2 on dev15 and add the manual-review step of the plan on a fixed sample; (4) only then write the deterministic definitions of criteria 1 and 7 to 9 into the protocol and consider freezing dev16. I-3 (model-written phrasing) stays deferred until those results are reviewed, and should proceed only if it shows a measurable benefit over the template floor.
