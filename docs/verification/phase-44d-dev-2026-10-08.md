# Phase 44D development-split comparison, 2026-10-08

Retrieval configurations B1a, B1b and B1c measured on the **development split only** (25 cases, 21 with expected sources) of the [retrieval benchmark](../../services/companion-core/benchmarks/knowledge_retrieval/README.md). **The frozen holdout was not scored** for any of them; the harness refuses to (`KBENCH_HOLDOUT_APPROVAL` must name an approved decision point) and a test enforces it. Nothing here is wired into a route, a conversation path or a flag, and nothing was deployed. The comparison measures **retrieval only**: no model wrote or judged an answer, so retrieval quality and generated-answer quality are not mixed (answer-level evaluation does not exist yet). The corpus is small and synthetic and the labels are provisionally reviewed; these figures are not evidence of real-world quality.

## The configurations

| | Candidate generation | Fusion | After revalidation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1a** | PostgreSQL full-text search over the unified index (OR of the stemmed query words, `ts_rank_cd`) | none | none |
| **B1b** | B1a plus pgvector cosine search with the real all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embedding of the query | reciprocal rank fusion, k = 60, 40 candidates per leg | none |
| **B1c** | B1b | as B1b | cross-encoder (`ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`) reorders the top 20 **authorised** survivors, with the source text |

All three: candidates come from the index; **every candidate is revalidated against its authoritative store under the case's trusted `AccessContext`** (existence, visibility, the stricter of the index and source classification, scope, destination, ceiling) and the text returned is read from the store; the result is what the harness scores. The search also applies the caller's access as a pre-filter using the index's labels; the `-nofilter` variants switch that off to show revalidation alone. The reranker only ever receives authorised text (tested).

## Sensitivity of the hybrid

`results/44D-dev-b1b-sensitivity.jsonl`: B1b over four RRF constants (10, 30, 60, 100) and three candidate counts (20, 40, 100), twelve runs. R@5 stays 0.79 to 0.81, MRR 0.79 to 0.82, exposed unauthorised hits 0 in every run. The standard setting (k = 60, 40 candidates) was not tuned; the gap to B1a is structural, not a parameter choice.

## What this shows, and what it does not

- Making every source searchable through one index is where the gain is: B1a lifts recall@5 from 0.21 (today) and 0.74 (keyword overlap over everything) to 0.86 with stemming and ranking, at 8 ms median, with no unauthorised exposure by construction and by test.
- Adding vectors did **not** improve recall@5 here (0.79 against 0.86) though it raised MRR (0.80 against 0.66): it moves relevant parts higher when found and sometimes crowds out a lexical hit (one single-source case, D-S1, is lost). The two intervals overlap completely and 21 cases separate them by 2 cases. It costs three times the median latency and several times the CPU and memory.
- The cross-encoder is **not justified**: no gain over B1b in recall or MRR, five times the latency, three times the CPU and 160 MB more memory. It stays implemented, off, and not wired anywhere.
- Nothing here establishes that either configuration is better on real data, on the frozen holdout, or for generated answers. The development split informed the design of these runs (cases were visible), so it is not an unbiased estimate; the holdout is.
